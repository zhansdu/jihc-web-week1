AlphaZero defeats Stockfish after training itself from scratch in just hours, reshaping how the game is studied

The Big Trio

Pin

A piece is attacked, but it can't move because doing so would expose a more valuable piece behind it (often the king) to attack. An "absolute pin" is against the king — the pinned piece legally cannot move at all. A "relative pin" is against a less valuable piece — it can move, but doing so would lose material.

Skewer

The reverse of a pin. A valuable piece is attacked and forced to move, exposing a less valuable piece behind it to be captured. Think of it as a pin flipped — high-value piece in front, lower-value piece behind, both on the same line.

Fork

A single piece attacks two (or more) enemy pieces at the same time, so the opponent can only save one. Knights are especially good at this because of their unusual movement pattern — a knight fork on a king and queen is one of the most common tactical wins in chess.
